Why I'm Building in Public

building philosophy

There's a certain vulnerability in showing work that isn't finished. The instinct is to polish, to perfect, to wait until something is "ready" before letting anyone see it.

But here's what I've learned: ready is a moving target. And while you're chasing it, you're missing out on the most valuable part of creating—the feedback loop that shapes what you're building.

The Case for Sharing Early

When you build in public, you create accountability. Not the oppressive kind that makes you anxious, but the generative kind that keeps you moving forward. You've made a promise, even if just to yourself, that this thing will exist.

More importantly, you invite others into the process. And sometimes—often, actually—they see things you've missed. They ask questions that reshape your approach. They point out blind spots you didn't know you had.

The Fear Is Real

I won't pretend the fear isn't there. Every time I share something unfinished, there's a voice that says:

What if it's not good enough? What if someone has already done this better? What if I look foolish?

That voice is protecting something—probably my ego. But it's not protecting the work. And the work is what matters.

What I'm Building

Right now, I'm working on DailyScribe—a system that takes voice memos and transforms them into refined content while preserving the original voice. It's part of a larger project called ALOS (Agent-based Life Operating System) that I've been developing.

Is it done? No. Is it perfect? Definitely not. But it's real, and it's making progress, and sharing that progress is part of how it gets better.

The Invitation

If you're building something—anything—consider sharing it before it feels ready. You might be surprised by what happens. At worst, you'll get practice being vulnerable. At best, you'll get insights that make your work significantly better.

Either way, you'll be building. And that's the point.
